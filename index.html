How it works

Expected change

For every pair of consecutive years, the tool fits one regression on your data: change = grade entered + prior level + prior level² + school year. The prediction is the change typical for a student like this one; the residual is how far the student's actual change sat from it.

Why condition

Raw gains on grade-relative proficiency scales shrink as students move up grades and levels. A fixed rule such as "less than 0.30 levels a year" therefore flags mostly older students and higher-level students, not necessarily students who are falling behind their peers.

Reading the flag

A flag means a student's change was lower than that of similar students in this file. It is a prompt to look at the student's instruction and context together with teachers, not a prediction, diagnosis or placement decision.

Limits

  • Annual changes carry substantial measurement error; one low year is often noise. Prefer students with two or more changes, and look at the trend.
  • In a prospective test with one district's records, conditional growth was associated with later state reading scores but did not add significantly beyond the prior year's reading score (Vieytes Cichero, under review). Treat flags as descriptive.
  • Estimates are only as good as your file: very small grades or years make the expected values unstable. The tool warns below 150 changes or 15 changes in a grade.
